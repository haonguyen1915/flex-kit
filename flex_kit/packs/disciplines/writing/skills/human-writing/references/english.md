# English AI-writing tells

The full catalogue, adapted from Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing. Each entry is a pattern to
avoid, with a fix. Read this when writing English prose for a human.

## Word choice

**AI vocabulary.** These words cluster in LLM output; one is fine, a pile of them is the
tell: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vital, vibrant, intricate, meticulous,
underscore, showcase, emphasize, foster, garner, boast, landscape (figurative), interplay,
enduring, "align with", "a rich history/tradition", "a wide array of".
- Fix: use the plain word. "look at", not "delve into". "show", not "showcase". "careful",
  not "meticulous".

**Elegant variation.** Swapping in synonyms to avoid repeating a word ("the constraints...
the limitations... the restrictions...") reads as strain. Repeat the word.

## Sentence shapes

**Avoiding "is/are".** Replacing the copula with "serves as / stands as / represents /
functions as / operates as / marks / boasts / features / offers".
- Before: "Gallery 825 serves as the association's exhibition space."
- After: "Gallery 825 is the association's exhibition space."

**Trailing "-ing" significance clause.** A participle phrase tacked on to editorialize.
- Before: "The library opened in 1901, cementing the town's role as a cultural hub."
- After: "The library opened in 1901."

**Negative parallelism.** "Not only X but also Y", "It's not X, it's Y", "not X, but rather
Y", "X rather than Y" as a rhetorical device.
- Before: "It's not just a tool, it's a philosophy."
- After: say what it is. "It's a tool for X." If the contrast is real, state both plainly.

**Rule of three.** Triads by reflex - three adjectives, three-item lists, three parallel
clauses - regardless of the content.
- Before: "fast, reliable, and scalable"
- After: use the count the content warrants. Often one precise word beats three vague ones.

## Content and framing

**Inflated significance / legacy.** "plays a crucial/pivotal role", "is a testament to",
"stands as a symbol of", "marks a turning point", "represents a shift", "underscores the
importance of", "setting the stage for", "leaving a lasting legacy".
- Fix: state the fact; drop the significance gloss. Let the reader judge importance.

**Promotional language.** "vibrant", "rich cultural heritage", "breathtaking", "nestled in",
"renowned", "groundbreaking", "a diverse array", "state-of-the-art", "commitment to".
- Fix: concrete detail, not adjectives. "founded in 1998 with 12 staff", not "a renowned
  institution with a rich history".

**Vague attribution / overgeneralized opinion.** "experts argue", "observers have noted",
"critics say", "industry reports suggest", "it is widely regarded as", "several sources".
- Fix: name the source, or drop the claim. One source is not "widely regarded".

**Outline-style conclusion.** The "Despite its X, it faces several challenges..." formula, or
sections titled "Challenges and Legacy" / "Future Prospects" / "Future Outlook" that hedge
toward a vague upbeat ending.
- Fix: end on something concrete, or don't add a conclusion at all.

**Section-summary sign-off.** Ending with "In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall", or a
paragraph that restates what you just wrote.
- Fix: stop when the content is done. A short piece needs no summary of itself.

## Conversational / chatbot tells

For replies, comments, correspondence - not just documents.
- Openers/closers: "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Sure!", "Great question!", "You're
  absolutely right!", "I hope this helps", "Happy to help!".
- Offers of more: "Would you like me to...", "Is there anything else...", "Let me know
  if...", "Feel free to...".
- Preambles: "Here is a...", "Below is a detailed breakdown of...".
- Refusal boilerplate: "As an AI language model", "As a large language model", "I'm sorry,
  but I cannot...".
- Knowledge-gap hedging: "based on available information", "as of my last update", "while
  specific details are limited", "not widely documented", and speculative bio filler like
  "maintains a low profile" / "keeps personal details private".
- Fix: answer directly and stop. If you don't know, say what you'd check, not that "details
  are limited".

## Formatting

- **Em dashes** everywhere. Use commas, parentheses, or a full stop. An occasional em dash
  is fine; a shower of them is the tell.
- **Curly quotes/apostrophes** where the surrounding text uses straight ones - match the
  context.
- **Emoji as bullets or section markers.**
- **Title Case On Headings** - use sentence case unless the house style says otherwise.
- **Bold on half the sentence.** Bold the term being defined, not the commentary.
- **Header-then-bullet-list** when two sentences of prose would read better. Lists are for
  genuinely list-shaped content, not for chopping up an argument.
- **Redundant title heading** repeating the document's name before the first line.

## The habit behind all of it

AI prose over-signals: it tells you a thing is important, connects it to a broader trend,
and wraps up with a summary, because it's trained to sound complete. Human prose trusts the
reader - it states the fact, picks the honest word, and stops. When a phrase feels like it's
there to sound finished rather than to say something, cut it.
