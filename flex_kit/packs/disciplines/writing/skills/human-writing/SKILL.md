# Human writing

The goal is prose a careful person would actually write. Large language models leave a
recognisable residue - inflated significance, hedged attributions, tidy triads, a summary
paragraph that repeats what you just read. Readers notice, and it reads as filler. Write the
draft, then reread it as an editor and cut the residue.

## How to use this

1. Write the draft normally - say the real thing.
2. Reread once looking only for the tells below. Most AI residue shows up on the second
   pass, not the first.
3. Load the reference for the language you're writing in and check against its list:
   - English -> [references/english.md](references/english.md)
   - Vietnamese -> [references/vietnamese.md](references/vietnamese.md)
   The tells differ by language; don't apply the English list to Vietnamese prose or vice
   versa. If you're writing both, check each part against its own reference.

## The core moves (apply in any language)

These are the ones that bite most often. The references have the full catalogue.

- **Say "is", not "serves as".** Plain copula over "serves as / stands as / represents /
  functions as / marks". If a thing is the exhibition space, it *is* the exhibition space.
- **Don't inflate significance.** Drop "plays a crucial/pivotal role", "is a testament to",
  "marks a turning point", "underscores the importance of". State what happened; let the
  reader weigh it.
- **Kill the trailing "-ing" tag.** Sentences ending in "..., highlighting its importance",
  "..., reflecting a broader trend" are almost always AI. End the sentence at the fact.
- **No negative parallelism.** "Not just X, but Y", "It's not X, it's Y", "X rather than Y"
  as a rhetorical frame. Say the positive thing directly.
- **Break the rule of three.** Not every list needs exactly three items, not every noun
  needs three adjectives. Use the number of items the content actually has.
- **Repeat a word instead of hunting synonyms.** Elegant variation (calling the same thing
  three different names to avoid repetition) reads worse than just repeating it.
- **Cut promo adjectives and vague attribution.** No "vibrant / rich / renowned / nestled",
  no "experts argue / observers note / several sources" standing in for a real source.
- **Don't sign off with a summary.** No "In summary / In conclusion / Overall" paragraph
  that restates the piece. Stop when you've said it.
- **Formatting restraint.** No em-dash sprinkled everywhere, no emoji as bullets, no Title
  Case On Every Heading, no bold on half the sentence, no header-then-bullet-list when a
  sentence or two of prose is clearer.

## Chat and correspondence, too

The same residue shows up in replies, not just documents. Skip the chatbot furniture:
"Certainly!", "Of course!", "You're absolutely right!", "I hope this helps", "Would you
like me to...", "Is there anything else", "Let me know if...", "Here is a...". Answer, then
stop. No "As an AI language model" disclaimers, no "based on available information" hedging
when you can just check.

## Why this matters

The point isn't a banned-word list to route around - it's the underlying habit. AI prose
over-signals ("this is important, this connects to a broader trend") because it's trained to
sound comprehensive. Human writing trusts the reader: state the fact, pick the honest word,
don't decorate. When you catch yourself reaching for a phrase because it sounds finished,
that's the tell - say the plainer thing instead.

The references list the specific patterns and give before/after fixes. Read the one for your
language when you want the full set.
